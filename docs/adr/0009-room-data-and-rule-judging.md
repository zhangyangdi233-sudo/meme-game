# A basement room is data; the rule engine only reads the sentence

Status: accepted

Each basement round has one room in `content/basement_rooms.json`, read through Framework `ContentJson` by `RoomContent`. A room lists its words per language, its optional vocabulary, the rules it offers, its escapes, its state (`brightness`, `hum`, `objects`) and its one-shot events. A planner changes a room by editing that file; `RoomContent.validate` fails a test on an authoring slip.

**The rules live in the floor's rule box.** The file is one floor: a `rule_box` holds every rule of the floor, each written once (`id`, subject, predicate, polarity, wording and the state it lays over), and a room's `rules` names by id the ones it offers. `RoomContent.room` hands callers the room with those ids replaced by the box's rules, in the room's order, so `RoomRules` and everything after it read a room exactly as before. A rule two rounds offer is one rule, changed in one place. Nothing in the shape names the basement: a later floor is another file of the same shape, and `RoomContent` takes its path and its room count (#119 Q3; a test reads a second floor's fake data). Validation checks the box once (shape, unique ids, barred subjects) and each room's ids against it; a room naming a rule the box lacks, or naming one twice, fails. Whether a rule can be written stays a room check, since it depends on the room's words and vocabulary.

Judging has two steps with a fixed boundary. `RuleEngine.read_sentence` answers what the sentence says: subject, predicate and polarity, in any word order, through the engine's word tables plus the room's vocabulary. `RoomRules.judge` answers whether that is a rule here: it looks the reading up in the rules the room offers, then falls back to a loose match that accepts a rule whose words all lie somewhere in the sentence. A hit is tier `rule`, a red post. Everything else stays `misread` or `noise`, a green post, and changes nothing.

**Rule keys stay `subject|predicate`.** `灯亮` and `灯不亮` are two rules of a room but one key, so the newest post decides, as it already did on the tower. Under the default pollution policy the second cannot follow the first in a basement round, since its subject is polluted.

**No second copy of what the world looks like.** A red post writes the `world_rules` map, with the round it was made in. `RoomRules.world_state` derives the room from its data and the rules in force, so the save needs no field of its own, and a rule from another round cannot leak in. Leaving a round forgets its rules, because the next room starts from its own data. The tower's rules carry no round and stay.

**A used rule dies by pollution, not by a list.** #119 Q2-3's mechanism sits on top of the above: a red post pollutes its subject, and `PollutionPolicy` turns a later sentence on that subject green, in the next room and the same one. Nothing records used rules. The check runs after `RoomRules.judge`, which stays pure; a void sentence is a green post like any other. Pollution counts by synonym group, by the rule's subject, so changing a word or the language does not revive a rule. The 50% threshold #117 first planned stays as `void_ratio`, unused by default: with skeleton words clean, `门是出口` is only one unit in three polluted and a ratio would never stop it.

**The stage only displays.** The host hands the derived state to the Reality scene adapter as `room_state`, and `ChapterOneStage` turns it into lights and objects. A room can name only the objects in `RoomContent.OBJECTS`, each of which the stage builds. A new kind of object needs code; a new use of one does not.

**Words and rules in other languages.** The file keeps `zh`, `ja` and `en` fields from the start. An empty word list keeps the whole pool, and a rule already reads in every language through the engine's word tables, so a room is playable before its translation exists.

**The exit is room data too.** A room lists its `escapes`, and any one that holds delivers the round (`RoomRules.open_exits`). `delivery` is this round's post plus the NPC's conversation; `rule` names one of the room's rules in force. A rule changes the room either way; it opens the exit only where the room declares it. All five rooms started on `delivery`, so #120's answer changes the data, not the code: round one dropped it (#125) and rounds two to five followed (#127), so every round opens by its exit rules alone. A room without `delivery` has nobody to talk it open, so the stage stands its NPC only where the room declares it, which no authored room does now; the kind and its conversation stay for the NPC behind the door (#129), and tests play them on a fixture. A later way out adds a kind and the fact that decides it. The X-ray is the first (#127): an `xray` escape names an `object`, and the fact is that the player looked at it through the X-ray, held on X or framed by the hands. The host asks the adapter which of the room's X-ray objects are in reach while the X-ray looks and hands them to `MemeGameState.see_through_xray`; `RoomRules.open_exits` takes them as one more input, and the object is recorded in `exits` like any other, so the save and the stage need nothing new. Round five hides one at its clock, where #114's X-ray clue points.

**The words follow the pollution (#140).** A round no longer drops a fixed list. `WordDrop` gives the room's own words plus the words of the first `guaranteed_exit_rules` exit rules (the rules the room names as escapes) that pollution has not voided, so every round can spell three clean ways out and the player picks one. The number and the seven exit subjects are floor data; the floor also carries a `vocabulary` every room reads under its own, so a word like `楼梯` is written once. Since an open exit stops pollution, each round uses up at most one exit subject. Exit and other rules keep their subjects apart (the room's light rules moved to `房间`), and `子` joined the skeleton so `桌子` cannot void `椅子`. The content check replays the worst case per round rather than trusting the arithmetic, so a planner who changes a number or a word learns at once whether the promise still holds. The drop is derived from the saved pollution and needs no save of its own. Japanese and English have no exit words in their pools yet; until #131 writes their words they keep the whole pool and are not checked.

**The way out is a thing in the room (#144).** An exit rule in the rule box names its `object`, one of `RoomContent.EXIT_OBJECTS` (door, stairs, TV, table, chair, clock, light), so which thing a subject opens is data; the NPC's help (`delivery`) opens the exit door. `RoomRules.open_exits` answers the objects the escapes that hold open, and the chapter progress records them in `exits`. Only those objects lead on: the state refuses to leave by any other, and the stage answers F only at their places. The stage knows where each object stands; a room cannot add a new kind of object without code, but it can use any of the seven.

**Opening is remembered, not derived.** The room's look follows the rules in force, but the exit stays open once earned: `delivered` and `exits` in the chapter progress are saved, and taking the rule back does not shut them; a save that only says `delivered` opened the door, which is all a delivery opened then. A player who wrote their way out is never trapped by their next post. The saved field keeps its name, so older saves load unchanged.

The tower still uses `RuleEngine.parse` with `SUPPORTED_RULES`. It is out of the demo's path (`LEVEL_ORDER`), and rewriting it would serve nothing.

Events are stored and validated here (`id`, `trigger`, then whatever their cue needs) but nothing runs them yet. #114 plays them and records that they fired. `hum` is the same: stored, not yet heard, until #114 brings the sound.
